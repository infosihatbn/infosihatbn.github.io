Community Eye Clinics

Want your eyes checked? No referral letter needed. Just walk in to any of these Community Eye Clinics and you will be seen by an eye doctor (an ophthalmology specialist and their team).

Tap a heading to open or close it.

What is a Community Eye Clinic?

Community Eye Clinics are walk-in eye clinics held at health centres. The eye doctors come to a health centre near you, so the public can get a specialist eye review without waiting for a referral letter.

This service is unique to Brunei Darussalam. In most other countries, patients first see a general doctor, ask for a referral, and wait for a hospital appointment before they can see an eye specialist. In Brunei, you can walk straight in.

Who can come?

  • Anyone. It does not matter where you live. You can go to any Community Eye Clinic, even if it is not your own health centre.
  • Come if you have an eye problem, such as blurred vision, a red or painful eye, new floaters, or any worry about your eyes.
  • If you have diabetes, glaucoma in the family, or are over 40, ask the doctor how often you should have your eyes checked.

Days and times

Office hours: 7.45 am to 12.15 pm, and 1.30 pm to 4.30 pm.

Please come in good time before the clinic closes, so there is enough time for triage, a vision check and assessment by the doctor.

  • Rimba Health Centre: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday
  • Berakas Health Centre: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday
  • Sengkurong Health Centre: Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday
  • Muara Health Centre: Thursday
  • Temburong (PIHM Hospital): first and third Saturday of every month

After hours or an emergency?

  • After office hours, on Fridays and on Sundays: go to a hospital Emergency Department.
  • Any emergency: go to the nearest hospital Emergency Department straight away. For any other medical emergency, call 991 or go to the Emergency Department.

Your EyeCare Team

At the eye clinic you may see several different people in one visit. Each has a different job. Knowing who is who helps you ask the right questions.

Swipe the cards, then tap a team member to find out what they do.

Ophthalmologist (eye doctor)

An ophthalmologist is a medical doctor who has done further years of training in eye diseases and eye surgery.

  • Diagnoses and treats all eye conditions, from red eyes to cataract, glaucoma and diabetic eye disease
  • Prescribes eye drops and medicines
  • Does eye surgery, laser treatment and eye injections
  • Looks for signs of other illnesses that show up in the eye, such as diabetes and high blood pressure

Who you might meet: a Consultant Ophthalmologist (senior specialist) leads the team. You may also be seen by medical officers and residents, who are doctors working and training in the eye department under the consultant's supervision.

Optometrist

An optometrist is trained to test your eyes for glasses and contact lenses. This is called a refraction.

  • Measures whether you are short-sighted, long-sighted or have astigmatism
  • Prescribes glasses and fits contact lenses
  • May help with low vision aids for people with poor sight

In Brunei, optometrists at government eye clinics mainly do refraction. They do not usually diagnose or treat eye diseases. If they find a problem with the health of your eye, they will refer you to the eye doctor.

How to see an optometrist in Brunei

  • Optometrists are based at the Community Eye Clinics.
  • It is by appointment only. There is no walk-in service.
  • To book, contact your health centre and ask for an appointment with the optometrist.
  • BruHealth appointment slots are for seeing the doctor, not the optometrist.

Orthoptist

An orthoptist specialises in how the two eyes move and work together.

  • Checks children for squint and lazy eye (amblyopia), and plans patching treatment
  • Assesses double vision in adults, for example after a stroke or nerve palsy, or with thyroid eye disease
  • May fit prisms on glasses to help with double vision
  • Does measurements before and after squint surgery

How to see an orthoptist: by referral only, from the eye doctor or an optometrist.

Ophthalmic nurse

Ophthalmic nurses are nurses with extra training in eye care.

  • Put in eye drops, including drops to widen the pupils before the doctor examines you
  • Help in operating theatres and injection clinics
  • Prepare you for eye surgery and explain how to look after your eye afterwards
  • Teach you how to put in your eye drops properly
  • Are often your first point of contact if you have questions or worries after treatment

Ophthalmic assistant

Ophthalmic assistants (also called ophthalmic technicians) help with tests before you see the doctor.

  • Check your vision with the letter chart
  • Measure your eye pressure
  • Take scans and photographs of the back of the eye (such as OCT and retinal photos)
  • Do visual field tests (side vision) and measurements before cataract surgery

These tests give the doctor the information they need, so the visit can feel long. Bring something to read and allow a few hours, especially if your pupils are widened.

Optician

An optician is different from an optometrist. Opticians usually work in spectacle shops.

  • Make and fit glasses from a prescription
  • Help you choose frames and lenses
  • Adjust and repair glasses

They do not examine the health of your eyes.

What to bring

  • Your identity card
  • Your glasses or contact lenses
  • All your eye drops, and a list of the medicines you take
  • If your pupils are widened with eye drops, your vision may be blurry for a few hours. Do not drive yourself home: bring someone who can take you.

This information is correct as of 6 October 2026. Days and times can change, especially on public holidays.
