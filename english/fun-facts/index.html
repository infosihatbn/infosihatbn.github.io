Myths or Facts?

Test yourself! Read each statement and tap Myth or Fact. You will see the answer and why straight away. Can you get 4 out of 4? There is a new set of questions every day.

1Eating lots of carrots will improve your eyesight.

Myth! Your body needs vitamin A for healthy eyes, but only a small amount. If you already eat a normal diet, extra carrots will not sharpen your vision or stop you needing glasses.

Sources: [14]

2Sitting too close to the TV damages children's eyes.

Myth! It does not damage the eyes. But a child who always sits very close may be short-sighted, so it is a good reason to get their eyes checked.

Sources: [14]

3Wearing glasses makes your eyes weaker over time.

Myth! Glasses help you see clearly. They do not make your eyes weaker or cause eye disease. Your eyes may change with age anyway, with or without glasses.

Sources: [14]

4A cataract must be "ripe" before it can be operated on.

Myth! This is an old belief. A cataract can be removed as soon as it affects your daily life. Waiting until it is very thick can make surgery harder.

Sources: [14, 15]

5Looking straight at the sun, even for a short time, can permanently damage your eyes.

Fact! True. Staring at the sun without proper eye protection, including during an eclipse, can burn the retina and cause permanent damage. Never look directly at the sun.

Sources: [14]

6Glaucoma can steal your sight without any pain or early warning.

Fact! True. The most common type of glaucoma has no symptoms at first. Side vision is lost slowly, and many people do not notice until a lot is gone. Regular eye checks after age 40 catch it early.

Sources: [16]

7If you have diabetes but can see well, you do not need an eye check.

Myth! Diabetic eye disease is often silent in the early stages. Everyone with diabetes needs an eye check every year, even with perfect vision.

Sources: [17]

8Children will grow out of a squint.

Myth! A true squint is unlikely to get better on its own. Early treatment, often before school age, helps prevent a "lazy eye" and permanent poor vision.

Sources: [18]

9If you cross your eyes for too long, they will get stuck that way.

Myth! Your eye muscles let your eyes move in all directions. Crossing your eyes on purpose will not make them stay crossed.

Sources: [14]

10Rubbing your eyes hard and often can harm them.

Fact! True. Frequent, hard eye rubbing is a risk factor for keratoconus, where the cornea becomes thin and cone-shaped. If your eyes are itchy, see a doctor rather than rubbing.

Sources: [19]

11Reading in dim light damages your eyes.

Myth! Dim light can make your eyes feel tired, but it does not harm them. Good lighting just makes reading more comfortable.

Sources: [14]

12Using a computer or phone for a long time damages your eyes.

Myth! Screens do not damage your eyes, but long use without breaks can cause eye strain and dry eyes. Try the 20-20-20 rule.

Sources: [14]

13Eye exercises can fix your eyesight so you don't need glasses.

Myth! Eye exercises will not improve your vision or remove the need for glasses. They only help a few specific eye muscle problems.

Sources: [14]

14All babies are born with blue eyes.

Myth! Many babies are born with darker eyes. Eye colour can change in the first year as the iris makes more pigment.

Sources: [14]

15Wearing the wrong glasses will damage your eyes.

Myth! The wrong glasses will not damage your eyes, but they can cause headaches, tired eyes and blurry vision.

Sources: [14]

16Losing your vision is a normal part of getting old.

Myth! Many eye problems that come with age, like cataract, can be treated, and others can be slowed if found early.

Sources: [14]

17Sleeping in contact lenses raises your risk of a serious eye infection.

Fact! True. Avoid sleeping in your lenses unless your eye doctor says they are made for it.

Sources: [20]

18Glaucoma can run in families.

Fact! True. If a parent, brother or sister has glaucoma, your risk is higher. Get your eyes checked, especially after age 40.

Sources: [21]

19Cataract surgery is usually done as a day case, while you are awake.

Fact! True. It is usually done under local anaesthetic (the eye is numbed) and most people go home the same day.

Sources: [22]
